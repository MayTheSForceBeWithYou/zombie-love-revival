# 📖 Zombie Love Revival — Story Bible

> This document defines the world, tone, themes, and narrative structure of the game.
> All content creators and AI agents should read this before writing any story content.

---

## Setting

**Time Period:** 3 years after the initial outbreak ("Year 3 of the Fall")

**Location:** Millhaven — a mid-sized American city that was never fully evacuated. Parts of it are reclaimed, parts are wild, and parts are something in between.

Millhaven is a patchwork of:
- **The Green Zone** — walled survivor settlements with electricity and running water (sometimes)
- **The Gray** — abandoned suburbs being slowly reclaimed by nature and roamers
- **The Bloom** — a strange, overgrown district where the infection mutated differently, producing semi-aware zombies with odd behaviors
- **The Undermarket** — black market tunnels beneath the old downtown core

The world is not purely grim. People have rebuilt routines, culture, dark humor. There are street artists, impromptu concerts, terrible home-brewed alcohol, and competitive scavenging leagues.

---

## Tone

The tone of Zombie Love Revival sits at the intersection of:

- **Bittersweet warmth** — There is real joy, real beauty, and real love in this world despite the horror
- **Dark comedy** — Absurdism as a coping mechanism; the apocalypse has a bureaucracy now
- **Emotional honesty** — Characters are allowed to grieve, be scared, be petty, and be wrong
- **Quiet dread** — Tension comes from quiet moments, not jump scares

**Not:** nihilistic grimdark, torture porn, or relentless hopelessness.

The game should feel like talking to a friend who has been through something terrible and survived — funny, sad, occasionally profound, never falsely cheerful.

---

## Themes

1. **Love persists** — The central thesis: connection survives even radical transformation. What does love look like when the beloved is no longer fully human?
2. **Identity and change** — Are you still *you* after the infection? After three years of survival?
3. **Community vs. self** — Factions want your loyalty. Other people need your help. What do you owe them?
4. **Grief as ritual** — The game takes grief seriously as an ongoing process, not a cutscene
5. **Complicity** — Every choice in the resource-scarce world has costs. Who pays?
6. **Absurd normalcy** — People make grocery lists during the apocalypse. This is realistic and funny and sad.

## Gameplay Loop

### Core Loop (per in-game day)

1. **Morning — Base** — Check status, relationships, active missions; talk to companions
2. **Afternoon — Field** — Select one zone to explore/scavenge/interact with NPCs
3. **Evening — Story** — A narrative scene driven by the day's choices and relationship states
4. **Night — Rest/Event** — Optional conversations, rest bonuses, or random events

### Meta Loop (per chapter)

- Each chapter centers on a core dilemma or faction conflict
- Player builds relationships and makes resource decisions
- Chapter climax forces a major choice with lasting consequences
- Endings reflect accumulated relationship and moral state

## Example Endings

### "Still Here" (True Ending)
The protagonist and their zombie love interest find an uneasy peace. They don't "cure" the infection — they build a life alongside it. Neither fully human, neither fully lost.

### "The Harvest" (Faction Ending)
The protagonist allies fully with the Cultivators, sacrificing personal bonds for collective survival. Cold, pragmatic, quietly devastating.

### "Fade Out" (Grief Ending)
The protagonist lets go — releases their love interest to the Bloom and leaves Millhaven. A road trip into an uncertain future. Melancholy but not hopeless.

### "New Bloom" (Wildcard Ending)
The infection mutates again. The protagonist, through accumulated trust with the Bloom zombies, becomes something of a bridge species. Open, strange, oddly hopeful.

## Core Factions

### The Greenwarden Council
The governing body of the Green Zone. Pragmatic, bureaucratic, occasionally corrupt. They want order above all else. They are not villains — they are people who chose structure and now can't let go of it.

- **Symbol:** A green circle with a white leaf
- **Philosophy:** "Stability is survival"
- **Conflicts with:** Anyone who disrupts trade or challenges authority

### The Cultivators
A quasi-religious group that believes the infection is an evolutionary step, not a disease. They study the Bloom zombies, protect them, and occasionally commune with them. Creepy to outsiders; genuinely compassionate within their own framework.

- **Symbol:** A blooming flower over a skull
- **Philosophy:** "Growth requires death"
- **Conflicts with:** Greenwarden Council; anyone who destroys Bloom zombies

### The Rovers
Loosely organized scavengers, traders, and wanderers who reject faction allegiance. They are the black market, the couriers, and the chaos. Funny, dangerous, fiercely independent.

- **Symbol:** A tire track
- **Philosophy:** "Move or die"
- **Conflicts with:** Everyone, but are also useful to everyone

### The Quiet
A loose network of people who have given up on factions and just... live. Gardeners, artists, teachers, archivists. They maintain small libraries and grow tomatoes. Deceptively influential.

- **Symbol:** An open book
- **Philosophy:** "Remember who we were"
- **Conflicts with:** No one, which makes everyone slightly suspicious of them

## Zombie Lore

### Types of Infected

**Roamers** — The classic slow shambler. Three years into the outbreak they are desiccating and becoming less dangerous. Still lethal in groups.

**Runners** — Early-stage infected. Fast, aggressive, recently turned. These are the ones survivors have the hardest time facing — they still look like people.

**Bloom Walkers** — Found only in the Bloom district. The infection in this zone mutated to include a fungal-like layer. These zombies move slowly, can show rudimentary behavior patterns, and occasionally arrange objects. They are not sapient, but they are *something*. Cultivators believe they have emotional memory.

**Bonded** — Extremely rare. A Bloom Walker that formed a sustained bond with a living human during infection. They exhibit preference behaviors, protective instincts, and what observers describe as "recognition." The game's primary zombie love interest is a Bonded.

### Infection Rules (for narrative consistency)

- Transmission: bite or significant blood contact
- Progression: 6–72 hours for standard; Bloom mutation slows this
- The infected feel no pain after Stage 2
- Bonded zombies have shown Stage 2 arrest — the infection stabilized without completing
- Nobody knows why this happens. This is a core mystery.

## Emotional Design Goals

1. **Player should feel the weight of every relationship** — affection values are not just numbers; they manifest in how characters speak, look, and prioritize the player
2. **Death should matter** — when characters die, the game pauses and acknowledges it; no glossing over
3. **Humor should feel earned** — comedy comes from character, not from the game winking at the player
4. **The zombie love story should be moving, not gross** — the romance arc is handled with tenderness; body horror is used sparingly and purposefully
5. **Player choices should have texture** — even small decisions ripple; the world notices
